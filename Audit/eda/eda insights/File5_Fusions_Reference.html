Canonical cancer fusion spot check

BCR--ABL1, EML4--ALK, TMPRSS2--ERG all returned 0 hits when searched with exact -- notation. All three genes (BCR, ABL1, EML4, ALK, TMPRSS2, ERG) are present in the file individually. Likely causes:

  • Gene name order may be reversed (e.g. ABL1--BCR rather than BCR--ABL1).
  • These specific cell lines may not carry those canonical fusions — not all cancer fusions occur in all cancer cell lines.
  • Not a data quality concern — requires loose gene-name search at query time rather than exact fusion name match.

Role in CellLineFinder

  • Fusions are contextual flags, not expression evidence. They do not contribute to the statistical confidence score.
  • Primary use: when a user queries a gene, check whether any high-confidence fusion involving that gene exists in candidate cell lines.
  • Surface in the AI evidence card as a trade-off or confound flag.
  • Example: a cell line highly expresses EGFR but also carries an in-frame EGFR fusion — flag this as a potential confound for the researcher.
  • In-frame fusions are the highest priority to flag. Out-of-frame and unknown reading frame are lower priority.
  • High fusion burden (cross-reference File 14 CIN/aneuploidy) may indicate a genomically unstable line — worth flagging in evidence card.

Known pitfalls

  • Always filter IsDefaultEntryForModel == 'Yes' first — 6,196 duplicate rows otherwise.
  • ENSG IDs include version suffixes (.21) — must strip before joining to golden key.
  • 13,503–28,600 rows have no ENSG for one or both partners — need decision on handling.
  • Canonical fusion name search must be gene-name-aware, not exact string match — gene order in name is not standardised.
  • tags and retained_protein_domains are entirely uninformative — do not attempt to use.
  • Most fusions (median FFPM 0.076) have very low read support — raw counts without FFPM filter will massively overstate fusion burden.
  • High fusion burden lines should be cross-referenced with File 14 genomic instability metrics.
