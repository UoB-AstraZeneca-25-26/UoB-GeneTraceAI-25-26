Orientation — important

This file is transposed relative to all other expression files.

  • Rows = cell lines (ACH- IDs). Columns = proteins.
  • All other files (HPA, DepMap RNA) have genes as rows and cell lines as columns.
  • At integration stage: melt to long format (ACH_ID, gene_symbol, protein_zscore) before joining.

Missing proteomics ≠ zero protein. Missing = below MS detection threshold.

  • Must be flagged as below-detection in the knowledge graph.
  • Never impute with zero or any value.
  • Never interpret missing proteomics as evidence of absence of expression — only RNA can indicate that.
  • At confidence scoring: missing proteomics lowers corroboration score but does not contradict RNA evidence.

Role in CellLineFinder

  • Corroborating evidence layer — proteomics confirms whether a gene detected at RNA level also has detectable protein.
  • RNA says "the recipe exists"; proteomics says "the dish was actually made."
  • High protein z-score + high RNA expression = strong evidence for target presence.
  • High RNA + missing proteomics = moderate evidence (protein may be present but below MS detection).
  • High RNA + low protein z-score = flag as potential post-transcriptional regulation — worth noting in AI evidence card.
  • Does not contribute to the statistical confidence score directly; feeds the AI rationale layer.

Known pitfalls

  • File is transposed — rows are cell lines, not genes. Easy to join in wrong orientation.
  • Missing values are below-detection, not zero — never impute.
  • 396 genes have multiple UniProt columns — collapsing strategy must be agreed before integration.
  • Isoform columns (-N suffix) will cause duplicate gene symbol joins if not filtered first.
  • 27.8% sparsity is normal for MS proteomics — do not interpret as data quality issue.
  • Tissue-specific proteins (e.g. KLK3, TPH1) are almost entirely missing across cell lines — expected.
